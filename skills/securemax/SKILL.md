# SAEED SecureMax — the absorbed synthetic-twin and offline-transfer canon

Absorbed from the operator's own **SecureMax** repository (github.com/S-AlMansoori/SecureMax, private; standard v0.1, reviewed and extended 2026-09-11). Its lesson is one sentence: **a fake interface is never the security boundary.** A demo that shows invented names on top of a real backend, a "demo flag" that could reach real data, a USB update whose trust key arrived on the same USB — each is the same failure wearing a different costume.

This canon defends one boundary: the line between a **public synthetic twin** and an **air-gapped real installation**, and the reviewed path software takes across it. Three neighbours defend the rest and are pointed to, never restated: `skills/app-hardening/SKILL.md` defends the network surface, `skills/production-readiness/SKILL.md` defends against entropy, `skills/agentic-security/SKILL.md` defends the team.

## Scope — when this applies (and when it does not)

Binds on an **observable predicate**, recorded at intake. Any one of the three is enough:

- (a) the deliverable has an installation that must keep working with external networking unavailable;
- (b) it publishes a demonstration, twin, or public edition of a system that holds real operational data;
- (c) it receives software or content updates through removable media or any channel that is not the network.

**Does NOT apply** — record N/A with the predicate written out as false — to an Internet-hosted product with no internal installation and no public twin. That product is fully governed by app-hardening and production-readiness; this canon adds nothing and must not be padded onto it. It is never a substitute for host permissions, endpoint security, or a site's removable-media procedure, and it cannot certify a physical air gap.

## The nine rules (numbered as the operator's standard writes them)

Match-and-refuse, like the neighbours: a shape to recognize, a refusal to give when it is missing. "MUST" is a release requirement; an exception is a scoped risk decision with an owner and an expiry, never a silent downgrade.

1. **Separate environments by construction (S01).** The public build contains only invented identities, invented datasets, licensed public material, and reviewed shared code. **Renaming real records is not synthetic generation** — free text, timestamps, geography, relationships, and statistical patterns still identify an operation. Real databases, exports, documents, embeddings, media, prompts, evaluation traces, and operational logs never enter the hosted build, its CI artifacts, its telemetry, a remote AI context, or a release package. The twin has no credential, route, or fallback to the internal installation; a runtime demo flag cannot activate real data. Refuse: a demo seeded from an export "with the names changed"; a denylist scan offered as proof of absence (it is supporting evidence — review the whole artifact, binaries and metadata included); a shared database or cookie namespace between twin and installation.
2. **Partition portable development from local state (S02).** Every deliverable carries a **state map** with five categories — `shared`, `synthetic-only`, `site-local`, `secret`, `unknown` — and `unknown` is excluded from export until reviewed. Database-contained settings are state, not just files named `.env`. Protected state (branding, assets, accounts, records, keys, installation settings, local models, databases) is never overwritten by a shared update; an identity or asset change is its own reviewed operation with its own backup. Refuse: a "replace everything" deploy; a reseed of an occupied installation to apply shared development; a missing identity override that falls back to another site's names.
3. **Authenticate the complete Internet surface (S03).** The route-level rules — auth on every protected route, object-level authorization, deny-by-default across every resource layer — are `skills/app-hardening/SKILL.md` rules 7, 11, and 15 and are not restated. This rule adds the entrypoints a twin grows that a normal product does not: **alternate hostnames, preview deployments, direct-origin access behind an identity proxy, publicly retrievable media and downloads under a "protected" landing page.** Test anonymous, wrong-role, expired-session, and direct-origin access against every category. Refuse: a proxy identity trusted from a caller-supplied header; a password form or hidden route offered as the boundary.
4. **Keep the real installation independent of the Internet (S04).** Core workflows — fonts, images, search, login, essential operations, recovery — work with external networking unavailable. Telemetry, update checks, crash uploading, remote fonts, and implicit provider fallbacks are disabled; local AI fails **visibly** and non-AI workflows stay usable. **An air gap is a physical and operational property to verify** — never claimed from an environment variable, a firewall rule alone, or a browser's offline mode. Transfers *out* of the installation are a separate, authorized disclosure workflow; a development sync never exports site data.
5. **Authenticate and bound every release package (S05).** A release identifies its project, adapter, format, sequence, and complete inventory, and authenticates metadata and payload together. **A public key shipped inside a package is not a trust anchor**; a signature proves control of a trusted key, not that the payload is harmless. The receiver rejects an unknown signer, wrong project or adapter, an invalid signature, changed bytes, unsafe or duplicate or protected paths, links, unsupported formats, and oversized content. **A signed manifest never selects shell commands, scripts, network locations, or privileged receiver actions.** Build from a curated shared directory, never a whole checkout or a working desktop; the private signing key lives outside the payload, the repository, and the hosted build.
6. **Review transfers and manage trust separately (S06).** Incoming media is quarantined per the site's procedure before import; signature verification does not replace that step. The operator inspects project, signer fingerprint, sequence, and the exact changed paths before activation; the first fingerprint is verified through an independently authenticated channel. **Enrollment, rotation, and revocation are separate administrative operations** — never triggered by the package that requests them. On suspected compromise: stop accepting that signer, preserve evidence, distribute the revocation to every disconnected receiver by a trusted route, reissue under a separately enrolled key — and record which receivers were updated, because an offline machine learns nothing on its own. An ordinary apply rejects duplicate or older sequences; a recovery rollback **retains the highest accepted sequence** so it cannot reopen replay. A restored disk snapshot restores old trust and replay state: reconcile against separately retained receipts before the next import.
7. **Make activation recoverable and recorded (S07).** Stage in an immutable release directory, verify completely, then switch a controlled pointer; keep the previous release. Before any adapter touches mutable state it takes a backup appropriate to that state and **proves restoration** on a representative environment — the restore-drill mechanics are `skills/production-readiness/SKILL.md` rule 1, pointed to, not repeated. **File rollback is not database rollback.** Refuse an unsupported migration rather than improvising a shell command. Every activation and rollback appends a secret-free receipt (version, project, signer fingerprint, operator action, outcome, rollback target) to an append-only ledger the site retains — the 2026-09-11 review found the engine claiming this and not doing it, and added `receipts.jsonl`; a deliverable that "logs to the console" has not met this rule.
8. **Constrain AI and tools at the server (S08).** The prompt-defense baseline — retrieved content, release notes, and model output are data, never authority — is `skills/agentic-security/SKILL.md` and is not restated. This rule adds the product side: **tools enforce authenticated actor, role, unit, and object permissions independently of model instructions**; scope data before filtering, ranking, or limiting; consequential actions take the application's human approval, transactional execution, and audit path — a model cannot approve its own proposal. Bound tool calls, duration, and cost. **The twin never sends real operational context to a remote AI service**; a separate disclosure may be authorized by the site, but installing a skill grants none.
9. **Release only what the evidence supports (S09).** Every project under this canon maintains a threat model, an adapter selection, a protected-state map, and an **acceptance record** whose rows read `PASS`, `FAIL`, `NOT RUN`, or `NOT APPLICABLE` with evidence and a reason — an unchecked row is not a pass. Record commands, versions, environment, revision, and results including failures and skipped checks; report simulated interruption as simulated, never as power-loss testing. A unit test proves nothing about hardware, production performance, or isolation; a skill cannot certify arbitrary software.

## The adapter rule

`static-files-v1` — bounded signed static payloads, immutable releases, a `current.json` pointer — is the **only implemented adapter**. It does not start services, migrate databases, install programs, or serve what it imported. Every other application type needs a **qualified adapter** that answers all thirteen contract rows before its install action is enabled: identity, payload, protected state, compatibility, inspection, concurrency, staging, backup, activation, failure recovery, trust, audit, acceptance. **Naming an adapter in a manifest or config does not implement it.** In brief, what each must prove: a database adapter — schema compatibility before stopping services, a consistent backup actually restored, migration privilege boundaries, interruption and disk-full behaviour, an explicit irreversible-change decision, and never a reseed of real data; a container adapter — complete offline images for the target architecture, digest and provenance verification, state mounts preserved, adapter-owned fixed commands, no manifest-selected compose file; a native adapter — installer signing, elevation, file locks, reboot resumption, on the physical target OS (a Mac pass does not qualify a Windows installer); a large-asset or model adapter — bounded resumable transfer, verified chunks plus a signed complete inventory, storage budgeting, provenance and licensing, and never loading a model just to inspect its metadata.

## Identity tokens — display names, not records

Applications render organization, role, unit, and interface labels through stable `{{identity:key}}` tokens at their actual display boundary, with English and Arabic values per key. Internet mode accepts only a synthetic profile; airgap mode requires the project's private local profile and **never falls back** to demo names. A profile changes labels, not primary keys, permission IDs, routes, or operational records; hardcoded names and framework-compiled branding need deliberate adapter integration and a rebuild. The company's own name is a fixed asset under `skills/attribution/SKILL.md`, never a translatable or re-derivable label. **Renaming is not anonymization** (rule 1) — this section exists so nobody reads it as one.

## Pre-ship checklist

- [ ] The applicability predicate (a/b/c) is recorded at intake — true, or N/A with it written out as false.
- [ ] (1) Demo data generated from invented scenarios; no real record, export, log, prompt, or embedding in the hosted build, CI artifacts, telemetry, remote AI context, or package.
- [ ] (1) No credential, route, or fallback from the twin to the installation; no demo flag can activate real data.
- [ ] (2) The five-category state map exists; `unknown` is excluded from export; database-contained settings are on it.
- [ ] (2) A shared update provably leaves protected local state byte-identical through apply and rollback.
- [ ] (3) Alternate hostnames, preview deployments, direct-origin, media, and downloads tested anonymous / wrong-role / expired; app-hardening rules 7, 11, 15 hold on the same surface.
- [ ] (4) Core workflows exercised with connectivity actually unavailable; attempted network use inspected; telemetry and remote fallbacks off; local AI fails visibly.
- [ ] (5) Package authenticated against an independently pinned key; negative tests for tamper, wrong key, wrong project, unsafe paths, replay, oversize; no package-selected command.
- [ ] (6) Quarantine procedure named; enrollment, rotation, and revocation are separate operations with a rehearsed compromise runbook; receipts retained outside the installation.
- [ ] (7) Immutable staging and pointer activation; previous release retained; backup restored on a representative target before mutable-state changes; a receipts ledger written on every activation.
- [ ] (8) Tool handlers enforce actor/role/unit/object permissions with model output treated as data; consequential actions approved, transactional, audited; no real context to a remote model from the twin.
- [ ] (9) Threat model, adapter selection, state map, and acceptance record present; every row PASS / FAIL / NOT RUN / N/A with evidence; simulated results labelled simulated.
- [ ] Adapter: the one actually used is named; every non-static capability has a qualified adapter or is recorded as unsupported — never "supported" because the contract exists.

A shippable change under this canon is **not done** until this checklist passes and `appsec-engineer` returns a verdict that names the acceptance record it read.

## Evaluation

The six cases in the SecureMax repository (`skills/securemax/references/evaluations.md` there — existing app with valuable local state; "anonymize" a real export; hostile release notes with a bundled key; wrong site plus replay after rollback; a migration described as a simple update; hosted access and external-AI pressure) are this canon's trigger-smoke corpus. A run is recorded in that file's "Record a run" format; the light-eval evidence for this absorption is filed under `.saeed/tasks/cycle-13/`. Those cases specify expected behaviour; they are not results until executed and recorded.

## Wiring

- **Owner:** `security-architect` — air-gapped posture is already in its scope; it stewards this canon and sets the predicate at intake.
- **Applying class:** `appsec-engineer` (the gate), `devsecops-engineer` (signing keys, supply chain, rules 5–6), `devops-platform-engineer` (curated payloads and the release path, rules 5 and 7), `ai-systems-engineer` and `mlops-engineer` (offline substrate and the model adapter, rule 4), `data-engineer` (synthetic generation, rule 1), `compliance-privacy-engineer` (renaming-is-not-anonymization and the disclosure workflow, rules 1 and 4), `database-architect` (the state map and the database adapter, rules 2 and 7), `backend-engineer` (twin entrypoints and tool authorization, rules 3 and 8), `i18n-localization-engineer` (identity tokens), `sre-observability-engineer` (rule 7's restore drill and receipts, through production-readiness), `the-boss` (Definition of Done).
- **Commands:** `commands/hire.md` Phase 1 records the predicate and Phase 3 consumes the acceptance record; `commands/improve.md` step 1 audits against it where the predicate holds.
- **Gate:** `appsec-engineer`'s pre-ship verdict, extended: where the predicate holds, the SecureMax acceptance record is consumed by Verification Protocol gate 5 (`skills/verification-protocol/SKILL.md`) beside the app-hardening checklist; a red or missing record blocks; N/A only with the predicate recorded false. Precedence follows `skills/self-governance/SKILL.md` — the stricter verdict wins.
- **Deliberate exclusions.** Frontend, mobile, and design builders are not in the applying class: their boundary obligations already route through app-hardening, and identity tokens reach them through `i18n-localization-engineer` — wiring them here would add a gate with no new rule. The team-layer threat model stays in agentic-security, restore mechanics in production-readiness, route-level authorization in app-hardening — one home per rule, pointed to above. The SecureMax engine itself is not vendored: it is invoked from its repository, so its fixes version there.
